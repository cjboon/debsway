Wren Linux

Privacy is power. Keep yours.

Every day we pour our lives into services that promise to keep them safe: our mail, our money, our messages, our secrets. And every day those promises are sold, leaked, handed over or simply switched off. Those who would watch us count on one thing: that we won't bother. The lesson is old and simple. No one can be trusted with your secrets but you. Wren Linux is built on it: a digital fortress whose keys are yours alone, a disk sealed by a passphrase only you know, a browser locked in a cell of its own, and updates refused unless they carry one signature.

Inside the walls, freedom is a few keystrokes away. Learn to make passphrases no machine will ever guess, and keep your keys in a keyring of your own with Seahorse. Walk the open web and the onion web alike with Tor Browser: the libraries, newsrooms and forums that live beyond the reach of censors. Speak through Signal, end to end, where not even its makers can listen. Hold money no bank can freeze or watch, with Monero and Feather. It is the crypto-anarchist's old promise made practical: mathematics instead of permission, encryption no one has learned to break, in the hands of anyone willing to learn it.

A fortress is only as strong as the one who keeps it. Wren Linux makes the common attacks hard; it can't make you invisible, and against an adversary with a nation's resources hunting you by name, no laptop alone is enough. Know your walls before you trust them: read what Wren Linux doesn't do.
