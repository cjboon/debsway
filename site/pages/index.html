Privacy is power. Keep yours.

Your life is written down somewhere: in messages, photos, bank records, searches and the places your phone has been, most of it on computers you don't own, readable by the companies that keep it, by anyone who breaks in, and by anyone who can make them hand it over. Encryption changes who can read it. A disk sealed with a good passphrase is only noise to the thief who takes the laptop; a message sent end to end can be read by the one person it was meant for; a password manager turns a hundred weak secrets into one strong one. None of this is just for spies. It protects ordinary things: a stolen laptop that stays a stolen laptop and never becomes a stolen identity, a medical question no one else needs to see, a conversation that stays between friends. Wren Linux puts it within reach of anyone: a computer set up around an encrypted disk, a browser kept apart from your files, and the tools to encrypt the rest, each one explained in plain words.
